# Tracing Time

## The simple version

Tracing Time is a private family travel storybook.

It helps people take years of scattered travel photos, places, dates, and
memories and turn them into one organized family history. People can see their
travels on a map, group individual stops into complete journeys, add the people
who went with them, revisit a daily memory, share a private journey link, and
print a journey as a keepsake.

This is not mainly a trip-planning app. It is for remembering the trips that
already happened.

## Why we think it could matter

Most family travel history is spread across:

- phone camera rolls;
- old computers and hard drives;
- social media posts;
- paper notes;
- email messages;
- memories that only one person still remembers.

Those things are hard to search, hard to explain, and easy to lose. Tracing
Time gives a family one calm, private place to preserve the story.

The emotional promise is simple:

> Your family history, brought to life through the places, people, and moments
> you’ve shared.

## Who it is for

Our first customers should be families, not everybody who travels.

The best early users are likely to be:

- grandparents or retirees with many years of travel memories;
- parents who want to preserve family trips for their children;
- adult children organizing a parent’s photos and stories;
- families preparing for a reunion, anniversary, or milestone birthday;
- people who care about family history but do not want to build a complicated
  genealogy project.

The first version should feel especially useful to the person in a family who
already says, “I have all these pictures, but they are a mess.”

## What makes it different

The product combines several small jobs into one meaningful experience:

1. Remember a place.
2. Attach the story and photos.
3. Put the place in the correct journey.
4. See the whole history on a map and timeline.
5. Share one private story with family.
6. Preserve it outside the app with an export or printed book.

The important product idea is not “store photos.” It is “turn memories into a
family story.”

## What we already have

The current app already has the foundation for a usable demonstration:

- a dashboard with a rotating memory;
- individual trips and places;
- journeys that group multiple stops together;
- an interactive map;
- dates that can be exact, approximate, year-only, or unknown;
- travelers such as family members and friends;
- photo uploads, thumbnails, captions, and location information;
- duplicate and missing-detail cleanup tools;
- analytics about the travel history;
- offline support through the progressive web app;
- private journey links;
- print/save-as-PDF travel books;
- database and photo backups for the current family deployment.

That means the first business step is not inventing the idea. It is making the
existing experience safe, easy to try, easy to explain, and easy to support.

## What we are trying to do

We want to answer four questions before spending serious money:

1. Do other families understand the idea quickly?
2. Will they actually put their memories into it?
3. Which part do they value most: organizing, storytelling, sharing, or
   printing?
4. Would they pay for a private, lasting place to keep it?

We can answer those questions with a small group of invited beta testers before
building a large public service.

## How we can start without spending money

For the first beta, we can use the servers and services we already have:

- host the app on the existing Ubuntu server;
- publish it through the existing Cloudflare Tunnel;
- keep the database and photos on the existing server;
- keep encrypted backups in the existing backup system;
- invite only a few friends;
- limit the number of families, photos, and storage used;
- provide help personally instead of building every automated feature first.

This is a testing arrangement, not the final promise to paying customers. It
lets us learn whether the product is useful before buying infrastructure.

Free hosting does not mean unlimited hosting. If the beta grows, we may need to
pay for a cloud server, storage, email, backups, monitoring, or other services.
That is normal. The goal is for customer revenue to arrive before those costs
become necessary.

## What success looks like at first

The first success is not thousands of users.

It is five families who:

- understand the product without a long explanation;
- add real memories;
- return more than once;
- share at least one journey with someone else;
- say they would be disappointed if the service disappeared;
- are willing to consider paying for it.

If we reach that point, we will have evidence that the idea deserves more
investment.

## The long-term possibility

If the beta is successful, Tracing Time could become:

- a small household subscription service;
- a paid “we organize your memories for you” service;
- a family-history and keepsake product;
- or a combination of software and personal setup help.

We should not decide the final business model before people use the product.
We should begin with a useful, private, respectful experience and learn what
families value.

## The guiding principle

We are not trying to make another noisy social network or another vacation
planner.

We are trying to help families keep the stories that would otherwise become
scattered, forgotten, or lost.
